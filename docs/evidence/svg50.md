## Diagnosis and independent calibration

Coordinate model: page 320×200 points; native canvas and PDF raster 640×400 pixels;
scale 2 pixels/point (144 DPI); native DPR 1; default browser viewport 1280×720.
Both paths apply the same SVG viewBox scale 2 and the signature blue path's scale
0.9 (net 3.6 pixels/SVG unit). Root/viewBox translations and placement remain
unchanged. Signature round blue stroke width is 5.4 raster pixels. Canvas size is
independent of the browser viewport; no screenshot resizing participates in the gate.

Temporary probes (`/tmp/opencode/svg50-probe.ts`, `svg50-mass.py`,
`svg50-calibration.ts`) found:

- Explicit SVG `image.decode()` and changing intrinsic SVG dimensions from 320×200
  to 640×400 produced byte-identical Chrome 154 native RGB to the original capture.
  This rules out those decode/bitmap-size fixes for this failure.
- Inline 640×400 SVG screenshot also failed against Splash: mass delta 0.0304302881.
- The original blue ink mass is 6001.9490 native versus 6195.3961 Splash; red is
  657.8706 versus 670.9569. Cairo measures blue 6076.3137, red 656.4706.
- Spatial blue mass in rows 0–249: native 3564.6784, Splash 3663.5725, Cairo
  3580.5059. Rows 250–289 (including the long horizontal stroke): native 2437.2706,
  Splash 2531.8235, Cairo 2495.8078. The excess is not isolated to arc conversion.
- System Chromium 152 yielded both the known passing native raster and the Chrome
  154-like raster in the diagnostic sequence. Version alone is not a proven cause;
  this assignment makes no claim about the internal Chromium raster-path selection.

The independent oracle is a **butt-capped, horizontal blue segment of length 100
points** with analytic area, not the signature. At 144 DPI its ink mass must be
`(100 × 2) × (strokeWidth × 2) = 400 × strokeWidth`. Test six widths and three
vertical phases, including widths both above and below the signature's stroke.
Every pixel participates in the existing white-composited contrast mass definition;
there is no ROI or edge removal. Full matrices for both browsers are retained in
`svg50/{154,152}/svg50-area.json` and regenerated by the new test.

Splash's position-dependent stroke coverage is unsuitable as an area oracle here;
Cairo is within 0.66% of analytic area across **all 18 cases**, not just the
signature. This establishes the principled harness correction without requiring
production curve/stroke calibration. Native raster coverage is not universally
exact either: four Chrome 154 calibration comparisons still exceed the unchanged
visual tolerance against Cairo, and their `nativeCairo: false` results are retained.
The analytic test checks the PDF oracle, not a claim of general native subpixel SVG
equivalence. Extending that contract would require a separate bounded decision.

Comparator quantities are unchanged: bounds use contrast >3; the joint edge mask
excludes a one-pixel band around channel gradients >3 from interior comparisons;
foreground matching includes every ink pixel, with symmetric one-pixel positional
matching and hue checks. Mass uses all ink pixels and retains opacity sensitivity.
No edge-band, hue, opacity, or ink-mass assertion was removed or weakened.
